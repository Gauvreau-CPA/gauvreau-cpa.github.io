Notifications for firms running Clio and QuickBooks Online

Your systems already know. The person who should act doesn't.

Nothing in your firm is missing data. A matter closes, a payment lands, a trust balance runs low — it's all recorded, correctly, in one system or the other. It just sits there until someone thinks to go looking. Reconcile turns those moments into a message, sent to one named person, in the app they already have open.

What you're actually buying

A notification service, not another dashboard nobody opens.

Dashboards require someone to remember to look. Notifications don't. Everything below exists to make sure the right message reaches the right person at a moment they can act on it — and that nothing else reaches them at all.

Routing

Named people, not a firehose

Rules route by fee earner, matter, amount or account. A trust warning goes to whoever holds client money. A large payment goes to the partner. Nobody receives someone else's problem.

Channels

Where your firm already is

Slack, Teams, or email. Text for the short critical list. There's no new application to log into, no seat to buy for a paralegal who'd use it twice a month.

Cadence

Urgent now, routine at eight

Each rule gets its own timing. Fraud controls fire immediately. Receivables arrive as one digest at the start of the day. Realisation lands weekly. Nothing arrives at 2am unless you said it should.

Thresholds

Numbers you set and change

Every amount, day count and watched account is yours. When a threshold turns out to be wrong — and the first ones usually are — it's a field, not a change request.

Acknowledgement

Someone owns it, or it escalates

A notification can be claimed, and until it is, it's still open. Leave a trust warning unacknowledged past your window and it goes up the chain automatically.

Record

Who was told, and when

Every notification, acknowledgement, snooze and mute is logged with a timestamp. At month-end that's a trail you can point at rather than a conversation you have to reconstruct.

The part that decides whether this works

Alerting projects don't fail technically. They fail from noise.

Two weeks in, the channel is unreadable, everyone has muted it, and a genuine trust warning scrolls past unread. That's how nearly all of these end. So restraint is built into the product rather than left to your discipline — the default assumption is that any given message should probably not be sent.

Digest by default

Real-time is reserved for a deliberately short list. Everything else is collected and delivered once.

Repeats collapse

The same matter edited six times in an afternoon produces one notification, not six.

One switch per rule

Anything can be muted in a click, with the reason logged, so people mute a rule instead of the channel.

Quiet hours

Evenings, weekends and holidays hold everything except the list you've marked critical.

Suppressed messages are kept

We record what we chose not to send, so tuning happens against what actually occurred rather than guesswork.

Reviewed at week four

We go through what fired, what was ignored, and what should have fired. Ignored rules get cut, not defended.
